Commercial Real Estate FAQ الأسئلة المتكررة حول العقارات التجارية

Key answers regarding commercial leases, ownership rights, freezone regulations, and transaction paperwork.

What is the standard agency commission for commercial leases in UAE?
Under Dubai Land Department (DLD) and RERA practices, standard commercial leasing commission is typically 5% of the first year's annual rental value plus VAT. For commercial sales, standard commission is 2% of transaction purchase price from both parties unless otherwise negotiated.
Can foreign investors purchase freehold commercial property in Dubai?
Yes. Foreign nationals and offshore entities can own commercial real estate on a 100% freehold basis in designated investment zones, including Business Bay, Downtown Dubai, DIFC, Jumeirah Lakes Towers, and Dubai South.
What is required for Ejari registration on a commercial lease?
To register a commercial Ejari in Dubai, you will require: the signed unified tenancy contract, trade license copy (or initial approval certificate from DET), tenant Emirates ID and passport copies, landlord title deed, and recent DEWA premise connection number.
What is the difference between fitted and shell-and-core office spaces?
Fitted office spaces come equipped with finished flooring, false ceilings, lighting fixtures, and standard HVAC ducting, allowing rapid occupancy. Shell-and-core offices provide bare concrete structures allowing corporate tenants to execute bespoke interior branding and architectural fit-outs.
How are commercial service charges structured and paid?
In most Dubai commercial leases, building service charges (for common areas, security, elevator maintenance, and building insurance) are borne by the landlord, while internal utility bills (DEWA, chilled water AC, internet) are tenant obligations. We verify this explicitly in your contract.
Do you assist with commercial property acquisitions outside Dubai?
Yes. Emirates Commercial maintains operational licensing and designated commercial brokers covering Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, and Ras Al Khaimah for office clusters, retail strips, and industrial warehouses.
